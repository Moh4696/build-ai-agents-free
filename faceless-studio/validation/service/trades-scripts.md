# 30 ready-made video scripts for UK tradespeople

Use these for free sample videos and for paying clients. Each is 40–80 words, about 20–35 seconds of video.

**To personalise for a business:** add a last line such as *"Need a hand in [Town]? Message [Business name], link in bio."* Then put their logo, colours and handle in a brand kit.

**Rules:**
- No made-up reviews, prices, guarantees or awards. Only claim what the client confirms is true.
- Keep safety advice general. When in doubt, the message is "get a qualified professional to check".
- The 10 marked ⭐ have render settings and make up the portfolio samples (fictional, no real business).

To render: `node tools/render-scripts.mjs validation/service/trades-scripts.md --out renders/samples --only R1,R2,...`

---

## Roofing

### R1: Three signs your roof needs a check ⭐
<!-- voice: bm_george, template: listicle, mood: calm, caption: bold-pop -->
```text
# 3 Signs Your Roof Needs A Check
1. Slipped or missing tiles. Even one gap lets water in.
2. Damp patches on your ceiling or in the loft.
3. Moss building up along the edges.
Spot any of these? Get a roofer to look before winter does the damage.
```

### R2: Why gutters matter more than you think ⭐
<!-- voice: bf_emma, template: story, mood: corporate, caption: karaoke -->
```text
Blocked gutters are one of the cheapest problems to fix, and one of the most expensive to ignore.

When water can't drain away, it spills down your walls, soaks the brickwork and can find its way inside.

A quick clear-out once or twice a year, especially after autumn leaves, can save you a much bigger bill later.
```

### R3: Flat roof or pitched roof?
```text
Flat roof or pitched roof? Here is the simple difference.

Pitched roofs shed water quickly and usually last longer. Flat roofs are cheaper to build and great for extensions and garages, but they need good drainage and regular checks.

Not sure which suits your project? Ask a roofer to explain your options.
```

### R4: What happens during a roof inspection
```text
What actually happens during a roof inspection?

A roofer checks the tiles or covering, the flashing around chimneys and vents, the gutters, and the condition inside the loft.

You should get a clear explanation of anything they find, with photos if possible, before any work is agreed.
```

### R5: Storm damage checklist
```text
After a big storm, take five minutes to check your home from the ground.

Look for tiles on the lawn or path, bent guttering, or anything loose around the chimney.

Never climb up yourself. If something looks wrong, take a photo and call a roofer.
```

### R6: Moss on your roof
```text
Is moss on your roof a problem?

A little moss is common. But thick moss holds water against the tiles and can block your gutters.

Removing it gently, without pressure washing the tiles, is the safe way to keep your roof healthy.
```

### R7: Chimney check
```text
When did anyone last look at your chimney?

Cracked pointing, a leaning stack or damaged flashing can let water into your home, even if you never light a fire.

A quick inspection now can stop a small repair turning into a big one.
```

### R8: Getting a roofing quote
```text
Three questions to ask before you accept a roofing quote.

What exactly is included? How long will the work take? And what happens if extra problems are found once the old roof is off?

A good roofer will be happy to answer all three in writing.
```

## Plumbing and heating

### P1: Bleed your radiators ⭐
<!-- voice: bm_george, template: story, mood: lofi, caption: boxed -->
```text
Radiators cold at the top but warm at the bottom? That usually means trapped air.

Bleeding them lets the air out so hot water can fill the whole radiator again.

It takes a few minutes with a radiator key. If the problem keeps coming back, ask a heating engineer to take a look.
```

### P2: Boiler service before winter ⭐
<!-- voice: bf_emma, template: story, mood: calm, caption: bold-pop -->
```text
# Book Your Boiler Service Before Winter
Most boilers break down when you need them most, on the first cold week of the year.

An annual service helps catch problems early and keeps your boiler running safely.

Always use a Gas Safe registered engineer for any work on gas appliances.
```

### P3: Low boiler pressure
```text
Is your boiler pressure gauge sitting low?

Many combi boilers need around one to one and a half bar when the heating is off. Your boiler manual will tell you the right level.

If you have to top it up often, there may be a leak somewhere, so get it checked.
```

### P4: Frozen pipes
```text
Freezing weather on the way? Protect your pipes.

Keep the heating on low if you go away, insulate pipes in the loft and garage, and know where your stopcock is.

If a pipe does freeze, never use a naked flame to thaw it. Call a plumber.
```

### P5: Where is your stopcock?
```text
Quick question. Do you know where your stopcock is?

It is usually under the kitchen sink, but it can be in a cupboard, under the stairs or near the front door.

Find it today. In a leak, turning it off fast can save your floors and ceilings.
```

### P6: Dripping tap
```text
A dripping tap might seem harmless.

But a steady drip can waste a surprising amount of water over a year, and it often means a worn washer or cartridge.

It is usually a quick, simple fix for a plumber.
```

### P7: Signs of a hidden leak ⭐
<!-- voice: bf_emma, template: listicle, mood: upbeat, caption: bold-pop -->
```text
# 3 Signs Of A Hidden Leak
1. Your water bill jumps for no reason.
2. Damp or musty smells that won't go away.
3. Stains or bubbling paint on walls or ceilings.
Catching a leak early is always cheaper than repairing the damage.
```

### P8: Shower losing pressure
```text
Shower losing pressure?

Limescale in the shower head is a common cause, especially in hard water areas. Soaking it in descaler can help.

If the whole house has low pressure, it is time to call a plumber.
```

## Electrical

### E1: What is an EICR? ⭐
<!-- voice: bm_george, template: story, mood: corporate, caption: boxed -->
```text
What is an EICR?

It stands for Electrical Installation Condition Report. A qualified electrician checks the wiring, sockets and fuse board, and reports anything unsafe.

Landlords in England must have one for rented homes. Homeowners can get one too, especially in older properties.
```

### E2: Signs your wiring needs checking ⭐
<!-- voice: bf_emma, template: listicle, mood: dramatic, caption: bold-pop -->
```text
# 4 Signs Your Wiring Needs Checking
1. Lights that flicker or dim.
2. Sockets that feel warm or look scorched.
3. Fuses or trip switches that keep going.
4. A burning smell with no obvious cause.
Never ignore these. Get a qualified electrician to check.
```

### E3: Consumer unit upgrade
```text
Still got an old fuse box with wire fuses?

Modern consumer units include protection that can cut the power quickly if something goes wrong, which helps protect against electric shock and fire.

An electrician can tell you if an upgrade makes sense for your home.
```

### E4: Extension leads
```text
Extension leads are handy, but easy to overload.

Plugging high-power appliances like heaters and kettles into one lead can make it overheat.

If you rely on extension leads everywhere, you probably need more sockets. Ask an electrician.
```

### E5: Outdoor lighting
```text
Thinking about garden lights?

Outdoor electrics need the right weatherproof fittings and protection. That makes them a job for a qualified electrician, not a weekend project.

Done properly, they make your garden safer and look great all year.
```

### E6: EV charger at home
```text
Getting an electric car? Here is what to know about a home charger.

An electrician will check your supply, where the charger can go, and whether any upgrades are needed first.

Ask questions up front so there are no surprises on installation day.
```

### E7: Smoke alarms
```text
When did you last test your smoke alarms?

Press the test button once a month. Replace the batteries when needed, and replace the alarm itself when it reaches the end of its life.

Hard-wired alarms can be fitted by an electrician for extra peace of mind.
```

## Building and renovation

### B1: Planning an extension ⭐
<!-- voice: bm_george, template: listicle, mood: calm, caption: karaoke -->
```text
# Planning An Extension? Start Here
1. Decide how you will actually use the space.
2. Check whether you need planning permission or building regulations approval.
3. Get a few detailed quotes, not just a price.
A clear plan up front saves time, money and stress later.
```

### B2: Damp walls ⭐
<!-- voice: bf_emma, template: story, mood: lofi, caption: minimal -->
```text
Damp walls are not all the same.

Condensation, leaking gutters, failed pointing and rising damp all have different causes, and different fixes.

That is why it pays to get the cause properly diagnosed before you spend money on treatment.
```

### B3: Repointing brickwork ⭐
<!-- voice: bm_george, template: story, mood: corporate, caption: bold-pop -->
```text
Crumbling mortar between your bricks?

Repointing replaces the worn mortar to keep water out and keep your walls strong.

On older homes, the right type of mortar matters. Ask a builder who knows traditional brickwork.
```

### B4: Kitchen renovation order
```text
Renovating your kitchen? The order of work matters.

Plumbing and electrics first, then plastering, then flooring, then units and worktops, and decorating last.

Getting the order right avoids paying to redo work twice.
```

### B5: Garden wall cracks
```text
Cracks in a garden wall?

Small hairline cracks are often cosmetic. But wide cracks, leaning or bulging can mean the wall needs attention.

If in doubt, have a builder take a look before it gets worse.
```

### B6: Loft conversion questions
```text
# 3 Questions Before A Loft Conversion
1. Is there enough head height?
2. Where will the new stairs go?
3. Will you need planning permission?
A good builder will help you answer all three before any work starts.
```

### B7: Choosing a builder
```text
Choosing a builder? Do these three things.

Ask to see recent work. Get the quote and timeline in writing. And agree how payments will be staged, rather than paying everything up front.

A good builder will be happy to do all three.
```
